# Annotated Bibliography — RANZCP Psychiatry Training Redesign

Every citation below was verified against PubMed / publisher records (authors, title,
journal, year, volume, issue, pages, DOI/ISBN) via a structured deep-research pass.
**Corrections to commonly-misremembered details are flagged in bold.**

---

## A. CBME frameworks (the architecture)

**CanMEDS 2015 Physician Competency Framework**
Frank JR, Snell L, Sherbino J, eds. *CanMEDS 2015 Physician Competency Framework.* Ottawa:
Royal College of Physicians and Surgeons of Canada; 2015. ISBN 978-1-926588-28-5.
— The role-based outcomes vocabulary (Medical Expert + six intrinsic roles) and the explicit
alignment target of the RANZCP redesign. Fits psychiatry's reliance on the intrinsic roles.

**ten Cate on Entrustable Professional Activities (EPAs)**
- ten Cate O. Entrustability of professional activities and competency-based training.
  *Med Educ.* 2005;39(12):1176–1177. doi:10.1111/j.1365-2929.2005.02341.x
- **ten Cate O, Scheele F. Viewpoint: Competency-based postgraduate training: can we bridge
  the gap between theory and clinical practice?** *Acad Med.* 2007;82(6):542–547.
  doi:10.1097/ACM.0b013e31805559c7 — *Note the published "Viewpoint:" prefix and co-author
  Scheele F.*
- ten Cate O, Chen HC, Hoff RG, Peters H, Bok H, van der Schaaf M. Curriculum development for
  the workplace using Entrustable Professional Activities (EPAs): **AMEE Guide No. 99.**
  *Med Teach.* 2015;37(11):983–1002. doi:10.3109/0142159X.2015.1060308 — *It is Guide No. 99
  only (not "99/100").*
— EPAs translate competencies into entrustable units of work; central to the RANZCP CBFP.

**CBME: theory to practice**
Frank JR, Snell LS, ten Cate O, Holmboe ES, Carraccio C, Durning SJ, et al. Competency-based
medical education: theory to practice. *Med Teach.* 2010;32(8):638–645.
doi:10.3109/0142159X.2010.501190
— The canonical synthesis of CBME's rationale and pitfalls; read before committing to the model.

**ACGME Psychiatry Milestones 2.0**
*The Psychiatry Milestones* (2.0). Chicago, IL: Accreditation Council for Graduate Medical
Education (ACGME); 2020. Commentary: Thomas CR. The Psychiatry Milestones 2.0. *Acad
Psychiatry.* 2020.
— Concrete, observable developmental trajectories per competency; useful comparator for
defensible progression decisions.

**Programmatic-assessment / CBME perils synthesis**
Touchie C, ten Cate O. The promise, perils, problems and progress of competency-based medical
education. *Med Educ.* 2016;50(1):93–100. doi:10.1111/medu.12839
— From a CBME proponent; an honest catalogue of documented harms (burden, reductionism,
gaming). Use as a standing risk register.

**Implementation fidelity**
Van Melle E, Frank JR, Holmboe ES, Dagnone D, Stockley D, Sherbino J; ICBME Collaborators.
A Core Components Framework for Evaluating Implementation of Competency-Based Medical
Education Programs. *Acad Med.* 2019;94(7):1002–1009. doi:10.1097/ACM.0000000000002743
— Because most "CBME failed" stories are *implementation* failures, this gives fidelity
criteria for the rollout.

## B. Critiques of CBME (the map of failure modes)

**Andragogy critique** *(see also Section D)*
Norman GR. The adult learner: a mythical species. *Acad Med.* 1999;74(8):886–889.
doi:10.1097/00001888-199908000-00011. PMID 10495727
— *Do not confuse with Norman's separate 2000 piece "The adult learner: a misinterpreted
species?"* The citable basis for not making andragogy load-bearing.

**Reductionism**
Talbot M. Monkey see, monkey do: a critique of the competency model in graduate medical
education. *Med Educ.* 2004;38(6):587–592. doi:10.1046/j.1365-2923.2004.01794.x. PMID 15189254
— The classic charge that competency models atomise holistic professional practice.

**Competencies aren't independently measurable**
Lurie SJ, Mooney CJ, Lyness JM. Measurement of the general competencies of the Accreditation
Council for Graduate Medical Education: a systematic review. *Acad Med.* 2009;84(3):301–309.
doi:10.1097/ACM.0b013e3181971f08. PMID 19240434
— Empirical bite: the core competencies cannot be reliably measured as independent constructs.

**Milestones bureaucracy**
Norman G, Norcini J, Bordage G. Competency-based education: milestones or millstones?
*J Grad Med Educ.* 2014;6(1):1–6. doi:10.4300/JGME-D-13-00445.1. PMID 24701301
— Warns the milestones machinery can become administrative burden out of proportion to yield.

**Tea-steeping vs. i-Doc**
Hodges B. A tea-steeping or i-Doc model for medical education? *Acad Med.* 2010;85(9 Suppl):
S34–S44. doi:10.1097/ACM.0b013e3181f12f32. PMID 20736582
— The memorable metaphor for what CBME gains and loses; put in front of the committee verbatim.

**The competency discourse**
Whitehead CR, Austin Z, Hodges BD. Continuing the competency debate: reflections on
definitions and discourses. *Adv Health Sci Educ Theory Pract.* 2013;18(1):123–127.
doi:10.1007/s10459-012-9407-7. PMID 23053866
— On how "competency" became an unexamined orthodoxy, and the risk of reductionism.

**EPA over-enthusiasm**
Krupat E. Critical thoughts about the core entrustable professional activities in
undergraduate medical education. *Acad Med.* 2018;93(3):371–376.
doi:10.1097/ACM.0000000000001865. PMID 28857790
— Problematises the EPA bandwagon; relevant since EPAs are central to the CBFP.

**Assessment burden / surveillance (2020–2023)**
- Schut S, Heeneman S, Bierer B, Driessen E, van Tartwijk J, van der Vleuten C. Between trust
  and control: teachers' assessment conceptualisations within programmatic assessment.
  *Med Educ.* 2020;54(6):528–537. doi:10.1111/medu.14075. PMID 31998987
- Roberts C, Khanna P, Bleasel J, et al. Student perspectives on programmatic assessment in a
  large medical programme: a critical realist analysis. *Med Educ.* 2022;56(9):901–914.
  doi:10.1111/medu.14807. PMID 35373380
- Szulewski A, Braund H, Dagnone DJ, McEwen L, Dalgarno N, Schultz KW, Hall AK. The assessment
  burden in competency-based medical education: how programs are adapting. *Acad Med.*
  2023;98(11):1261–1267. doi:10.1097/ACM.0000000000005305. PMID 37343164
— The contemporary caution: programmatic assessment can be experienced as surveillance and can
undermine the very benefits of CBME. A first-order design constraint for the CCPR.

## C. The OSCE critique (Hodges)

**The pivotal expertise finding**
Hodges B, Regehr G, McNaughton N, Tiberius R, Hanson M. OSCE checklists do not capture
increasing levels of expertise. *Acad Med.* 1999;74(10):1129–1134. PMID 10536636
— Global ratings rose with expertise but checklist scores *fell*. **Correction: this study
used family-practice standardised-patient interviews, not a psychiatric station.** The finding
nonetheless underpins the case against checklist-bound station exams for relational competence.

**Validity & the discourse of the OSCE**
- Hodges B. Validity and the OSCE. *Med Teach.* 2003;25(3):250–254.
  doi:10.1080/01421590310001002836. PMID 12881045
- Hodges B. OSCE! Variations on a theme by Harden. *Med Educ.* 2003;37(12):1134–1140.
  doi:10.1111/j.1365-2923.2003.01717.x. PMID 14984124
- Hodges BD. *The Objective Structured Clinical Examination: A Socio-History.* Saarbrücken:
  LAP Lambert Academic Publishing; 2009. ISBN 978-3-8383-0181-5 (originated as a University of
  Toronto doctoral dissertation).
— The OSCE doesn't merely measure competence, it constructs a particular "competent performer."

**The constructive sequel (most useful for the redesign)**
Hodges B. Assessment in the post-psychometric era: learning to love the subjective and
collective. *Med Teach.* 2013;35(7):564–568. doi:10.3109/0142159X.2013.789134. PMID 23631408
— Rehabilitates expert judgement and narrative; the intellectual warrant for the CCPR.

**Hodges' psychiatry-specific OSCE work** *(where the psychiatric-interview evidence actually lives)*
- Hodges B, Regehr G, Hanson M, McNaughton N. Validation of an objective structured clinical
  examination in psychiatry. *Acad Med.* 1998;73(8):910–912. PMID 9736854
- Hodges B, Hanson M, McNaughton N, Regehr G. Creating, monitoring, and improving a psychiatry
  OSCE: a guide for faculty. *Acad Psychiatry.* 2002;26(3):134–161.
  doi:10.1176/appi.ap.26.3.134. PMID 12824132
- Hodges B, McNaughton N, Regehr G, et al. The challenge of creating new OSCE measures to
  capture the characteristics of expertise. *Med Educ.* 2002;36(8):742–748. PMID 12191057
— Confirms Hodges' substantial, psychiatry-specific assessment scholarship.

## D. Adult/formal education best practice & learning theory (the engine)

**Constructive alignment**
- Biggs J, Tang C, Kennedy G. *Teaching for Quality Learning at University.* **5th ed.**
  Maidenhead: Open University Press / McGraw-Hill; **2022.** ISBN 9780335250820. *(4th ed.,
  2011, if an earlier edition is cited.)*
- Biggs J. Enhancing teaching through constructive alignment. *Higher Education.*
  1996;32(3):347–364. doi:10.1007/BF00138871
— Outcomes, learning activities and assessment must align; the operational backbone of design.

**Curriculum design method**
Thomas PA, Kern DE, Hughes MT, **Tackett SA,** Chen BY, eds. *Curriculum Development for
Medical Education: A Six-Step Approach.* **4th ed.** Baltimore: Johns Hopkins University Press;
**2022.** ISBN 9781421444093. *(3rd ed., 2016, omits Tackett.)*
— The standard operational playbook for building a curriculum.

**Mastery learning & deliberate practice**
- McGaghie WC, Issenberg SB, Cohen ER, Barsuk JH, Wayne DB. Does simulation-based medical
  education with deliberate practice yield better results than traditional clinical education?
  A meta-analytic comparative review of the evidence. *Acad Med.* 2011;86(6):706–711.
  doi:10.1097/ACM.0b013e318217e119
- McGaghie WC, Issenberg SB, Barsuk JH, Wayne DB. A critical review of simulation-based mastery
  learning with translational outcomes. *Med Educ.* 2014;48(4):375–385. doi:10.1111/medu.12391
- Ericsson KA. Deliberate practice and the acquisition and maintenance of expert performance in
  medicine and related domains. *Acad Med.* 2004;79(10 Suppl):S70–S81.
  doi:10.1097/00001888-200410001-00022
— The learning science under CBME's promise; basis for simulation and interviewing/formulation
practice.

**Instructional design for complex skills**
- van Merriënboer JJG, Kirschner PA, Frèrejean J. *Ten Steps to Complex Learning: A Systematic
  Approach to Four-Component Instructional Design (4C/ID).* **4th ed.** New York: Routledge;
  **2024.** ISBN 9781032333113.
- Sweller J. Cognitive load during problem solving: Effects on learning. *Cognitive Science.*
  1988;12(2):257–285. doi:10.1207/s15516709cog1202_4
— Purpose-built for teaching complex professional skills such as assessment and formulation.

**Cognitive science of learning**
Dunlosky J, Rawson KA, Marsh EJ, Nathan MJ, Willingham DT. Improving students' learning with
effective learning techniques. *Psychol Sci Public Interest.* 2013;14(1):4–58.
doi:10.1177/1529100612453266. PMID 26173288
— Retrieval practice, spacing, interleaving: high-yield, replicated, under-used.

**Active learning**
Freeman S, Eddy SL, McDonough M, Smith MK, Okoroafor N, Jordt H, Wenderoth MP. Active learning
increases student performance in science, engineering, and mathematics. *PNAS.*
2014;111(23):8410–8415. doi:10.1073/pnas.1319030111
— Justifies designing contact time around application rather than transmission.

**Motivation (the robust replacement for andragogy)**
- Deci EL, Ryan RM. *Intrinsic Motivation and Self-Determination in Human Behavior.* New York:
  Plenum; 1985. — and — Ryan RM, Deci EL. Self-determination theory and the facilitation of
  intrinsic motivation, social development, and well-being. *Am Psychol.* 2000;55(1):68–78.
  doi:10.1037/0003-066X.55.1.68
- Ten Cate OTJ, Kusurkar RA, Williams GC. How self-determination theory can assist our
  understanding of the teaching and learning processes in medical education: **AMEE Guide
  No. 59.** *Med Teach.* 2011;33(12):961–973. doi:10.3109/0142159X.2011.595435
— Explains *when* autonomy helps; the evidence-based version of "self-direction."

**Situated learning / identity**
Lave J, Wenger E. *Situated Learning: Legitimate Peripheral Participation.* Cambridge:
Cambridge University Press; 1991. ISBN 978-0521423748
— How professional identity and tacit knowledge form in clinical teams; central to psychiatry.

**Assessment theory**
- van der Vleuten CPM, Schuwirth LWT. Assessing professional competence: from methods to
  programmes. *Med Educ.* 2005;39(3):309–317. doi:10.1111/j.1365-2929.2005.02094.x. PMID 15733167
- Miller GE. The assessment of clinical skills/competence/performance. *Acad Med.*
  1990;65(9 Suppl):S63–S67. doi:10.1097/00001888-199009000-00045
— Programmatic assessment (the CCPR's logic) and Miller's pyramid (aligning method to level).

**Faculty development (the highest-leverage investment)**
- Steinert Y, Mann K, Centeno A, Dolmans D, Spencer J, Gelula M, Prideaux D. A systematic
  review of faculty development initiatives designed to improve teaching effectiveness in
  medical education: **BEME Guide No. 8.** *Med Teach.* 2006;28(6):497–526.
  doi:10.1080/01421590600902976
- Steinert Y, Mann K, Anderson B, et al. A systematic review of faculty development initiatives
  designed to enhance teaching effectiveness: a 10-year update: **BEME Guide No. 40.**
  *Med Teach.* 2016;38(8):769–786. doi:10.1080/0142159X.2016.1181851
— WBA, feedback and entrustment all depend on supervisor capability.

## E. RANZCP-specific context & commentary

> Sourced from RANZCP web/PDF text surfaced via search (ranzcp.org blocks automated fetching)
> plus peer-reviewed and reputable secondary sources. Program/timeline details should be
> re-confirmed against the live RANZCP pages before formal use; flagged items noted.

**Program structure.** Competency-Based Fellowship Program (CBFP / "2012 Fellowship Program"):
60 months FTE over three stages — **Basic (Stage 1, 12 mo)**, **Proficient (Stage 2, 24 mo)**,
**Advanced (Stage 3, 24 mo)** — progression gated on time-in-rotation plus competency/EPA
attainment to the stage-appropriate entrustment level.

**EPAs.** Central to the program; roughly **25–30 EPAs** entrusted for Fellowship (exact count
varies by placement — confirm against current EPA handbooks).

**Workplace-based assessments (the five formative tools; ≥3 inform each EPA decision):**
Case-based Discussion (CbD); mini-CEX; Professional Presentation (PP); DOPS; **Observed
Clinical Activity (OCA)** — plus the newer **Independent OCA (IOCA)**. A **5-point rating
scale** replaced the 9-point scale for OCAs/IOCAs from August 2025.
> **Correction:** the **ACE (Assessment of Clinical Expertise)** and **mini-ACE** are **UK
> RCPsych** tools, **not RANZCP** tools. Do not attribute them to RANZCP. RANZCP's analogous
> observed-encounter tools are the OCA/IOCA and mini-CEX.

**Written/summative components:** MCQ Exam (140 MCQs + 2 Critical Analysis Problems); Modified
Essay Question (MEQ) Exam; Critical Essay Question (CEQ) Exam; **Psychotherapy Written Case**
(8,000–10,000 words, ≥40 sessions); **Scholarly Project** (3,000–5,000 words).

**The OSCE has been removed.** Suspended (COVID-19) → Alternative Assessment Pathway (Dec 2021)
→ Clinical Competency Assessment (Sept 2022) → CCA–Modified Portfolio Review (~2024) →
**Clinical Competency Portfolio Review (CCPR) from September 2026** — a **programmatic,
portfolio-based** synthesis of Stage 3 in-training assessments, OCAs and IOCAs. RANZCP
described the portfolio review as "robust and defensible" and better aligned with modern
assessment literature than the OSCE.

**Curriculum redesign (flagship).** First major overhaul in ~15 years; led with **ACER**
(2023–2024); driven by **AMC accreditation conditions** (curriculum structure, assessment,
cultural safety — notably AMC Standard 5, Condition 29). Realigns graduate outcomes to
**CanMEDS 2015**; new syllabus out for consultation in 2025 with **implementation targeted
~2028** *(timeline should be re-checked against the live redesign page)*.

**Published commentary / critical evaluation:**
- Amos A, Weightman MJ, Miller E. Modelling the rate of trainees transitioning to Fellowship
  before achieving competence under the RANZCP's Alternative Assessment Pathway to the OSCE.
  *Australas Psychiatry.* 2023. doi:10.1177/10398562231202120 — a quantitative critique of the
  OSCE-replacement pathway; the key "is the portfolio rigorous enough?" reference.
- Commentary in *European Psychiatry* on the RANZCP Alternative Assessment Pathway, and general
  medical press debate (e.g., AusDoc) on the OSCE removal — useful for acknowledging that the
  change is contested.
