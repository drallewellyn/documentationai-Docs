## 1. Schuwirth — Review of the Alternative Assessment Pathway (AAP)

**File:** [`aap-evaluation-external-review.pdf`](./aap-evaluation-external-review.pdf)
**Citation:** Schuwirth L. *Review of the Alternative Assessment Pathway of the Royal Australian
and New Zealand College of Psychiatrists.* 27 November 2022. (Commissioned external review.)
**Status:** Full text extracted.

**Purpose:** Commissioned by RANZCP to evaluate the validity of the AAP (the COVID-era OSCE
replacement) and advise the future program of assessments. The AAP = the three most recent
In-Training Assessments (ITAs, informed by WBAs/EPAs) plus a **portfolio review**.

**Key findings & relevance to the redesign:**
- **The AAP is "a better and more defensible assessment than the OSCE."** It is longitudinal,
  multi-instrument, multi-supervisor, in authentic practice — replacing a single-event,
  single-instrument, centrally-administered high-stakes exam.
- Grounded explicitly in **modern assessment theory** (programmatic assessment; domain
  specificity; competence as complex/non-linear; the legitimacy of human judgement; "assessment
  *for* learning"). Cites van der Vleuten & Schuwirth, Gingerich, ten Cate, Norman, etc.
- The **portfolio review process** (de-identified, firewalled panels + Portfolio Review Oversight
  Panel) was judged fair, holistic and defensible.
- **The one weak element: the Case-based Discussion (CbD)** used as a *resit* for a failed
  portfolio review — a single self-selected case overturning a whole portfolio is "less
  defensible" (domain specificity; trainee case-selection). Flagged as the first target for
  improvement (recommend multiple, non-self-selected cases; one examiner per case rather than
  consensus marking).
- **Everything hinges on supervisor "assessment literacy"** — recommends central assessor-training
  tools, communities of practice, infrastructure, remedial coaches, and *closing the feedback
  loop* between assessment moments (assessment *for* learning).
- Confirms program facts: 60 months (Stage 1 = 12, Stages 2 & 3 = 24 each); EPAs as outcomes
  (≥16 by end Stage 2; ~25–30 total); 5 WBA tools (mini-CEX, Professional Presentation, DOPS,
  CbD, OCA); learning outcomes mapped to **CanMEDS**; OCA on a 9-point scale vs other WBAs on a
  3-point ordinal scale (recommends dropping the 9-point).

*Why it matters:* the single most direct evidentiary warrant for the OSCE→portfolio shift, and a
ready-made "strong and convincing narrative" the College can use with external stakeholders.

## 2. ACER — RANZCP Examination Review (Final Report)

**File:** [`acer-ranzcp-examination-review-final-report.pdf`](./acer-ranzcp-examination-review-final-report.pdf)
**Citation:** Pearce J, Reid K, Chiavaroli N, Zoanetti N. *RANZCP Examination Review: Final
Report.* Melbourne: Australian Council for Educational Research (ACER); January 2020.
**Status:** Full text extracted (51 pp.).

**Purpose:** Commissioned by the RANZCP Board (engaged July 2019), initially in response to
consistently low **Essay-style exam** pass rates. Scope expanded to a "gap analysis" of three
Fellowship Examination components against best practice: the **MCQ**, the **Essay-style exam
(CEQ + MEQ)**, and the **OSCE**.

**Key findings & relevance to the redesign:**
- **The three exams together do not assess all core psychiatric skills.** Most strikingly, the
  **OSCE does not assess core clinical skills** (meeting/communicating with real patients,
  history, examination, diagnosis, formulation) — these moved to the formative **OCA** after the
  **Observed Clinical Interview (OCI) was removed ~2014**, which "negatively impacted the training
  program" (assessment drives learning; the formative OCA is under-valued and over-signed-off).
- **Training is functionally time-based, not competency-based** — progression is automatic by
  months FTE; trainees sit exams (e.g. OSCE) too early.
- **OSCE** (15 stations: 11 active + 4 byes): each station is a cursory sample; standard-setting
  (borderline-groups) is compromised by a "convoluted" rating→score transformation; uses a
  **compensatory** cut-score (recommend **conjunctive** rules); examiners struggle with the global
  proficiency rating; widespread disquiet that candidates pass who shouldn't.
- **Essay-style:** CEQ and MEQ function as two separate assessments (recommend separating them);
  the exam is **harder for non-English-speaking-background candidates** (an equity flag); marking
  guides need task-specific, principle-based rubrics.
- **MCQ:** broken-up EMQs harm authenticity; not well targeted to standard; could become a
  progression hurdle.
- **Cross-cutting recommendations:** integrate training and assessment; **incorporate programmatic
  thinking** (triangulate lower-stakes/OCA data for progression decisions); reintroduce an
  OCI-type observed assessment or triangulate OCA data; develop assessment frameworks (not just
  blueprints); formalise examiner training; simplify standard-setting; competency-based
  progression hurdles. Cites Hodges (2013), ten Cate & Regehr (2019), Schuwirth & van der Vleuten,
  Miller, Biggs, van der Vleuten's utility equation.

*Why it matters:* the foundational external diagnosis (pre-COVID) that seeded the assessment
reforms — and an independent corroboration of the OSCE's core-skills blind spot and the case for
programmatic assessment.

## 5. RANZCP Education Team — Burden of Assessment Literature Review (May 2024)

**File:** [`burden-of-assessment-literature-review-may-2024.pdf`](./burden-of-assessment-literature-review-may-2024.pdf)
**Citation:** RANZCP Education Team. *Review of Concept of Burden of Assessment — Review of
Literature.* RANZCP; May 2024.
**Status:** Full text extracted (8 pp.).

**Purpose:** Responds directly to **AMC Standard 5, Condition 23** — "systematically review the
breadth of assessment methods with a view to reducing the **burden of assessment** on trainees
and their supervisors… [and] determine reasons for the high prevalence of breaks in training
undertaken to complete summative assessments." Seeks an evidence-based, *measurable* approach to
burden of assessment.

**Key findings & relevance:**
- A non-systematic review of **10 papers** (mostly Canadian CBME residency programs, largely the
  trainee/learner perspective) — full citations in §5a below.
- **Ott et al.'s Self-Determination Theory lens** (threats to Autonomy, Relatedness, Competence)
  is adopted as the recommended **framework for conceptualising and measuring** burden of
  assessment — and the review proposes a draft **SDT-structured survey** (autonomy / relatedness /
  competence items) for trainees, supervisors and DoTs. *(This ties the burden-of-assessment work
  directly to SDT — see [Pedagogy §3](../redesign-report.md) and AMEE Guide No. 59.)*
- Recurring themes: administrative/time burden; learning overshadowed by assessment; variable
  feedback quality; **"gaming the system"**; the value of faculty engagement and coaching.
- **Implications for RANZCP:** shift from "1–2 EPAs per 6-month rotation" (a disconnected model)
  toward a set number of **entrustability assessments + discussions**; prioritise *useful feedback
  tied to coaching* over collecting ratings; train and support **supervisors to be proactive**
  rather than placing initiation burden on trainees.

### 5a. The 10 Burden-of-Assessment papers (stored in [`boa-papers/`](./boa-papers/))

1. **Szulewski A, Braund H, Dagnone DJ, et al.** The assessment burden in competency-based medical
   education: how programs are adapting. *Acad Med.* 2023;98(11):1261–1267. *(also in
   [bibliography §B](../annotated-bibliography.md))*
2. **Ott MC, Pack R, Cristancho S, Chin M, Van Koughnett JA, Ott M.** "The most crushing thing":

   2022;14(5):583–592. — *the key SDT-based conceptualisation of burden.*
3. **Cheung K, Rogoza C, Chung AD, Kwan BY.** Analyzing the administrative burden of
   competency-based medical education. *Can Assoc Radiol J.* 2021;73(2):299–304. — *empirical:
   mean EPA form ≈ 3 min 6 sec (but excludes pre-/post- discussion time).*
4. **Branfield Day L, Colbourne T, Ng A, et al.** A qualitative study of Canadian resident
   experiences with competency-based medical education. *Can Med Educ J.* 2022.
5. **Mann S, Hastings Truelove A, Beesley T, Howden S, Egan R.** Resident perceptions of
   competency-based medical education. *Can Med Educ J.* 2020.
6. **Ahn E, LaDonna KA, Landreville JM, Mcheimech R, Cheung WJ.** Only as strong as the weakest
   link: resident perspectives on EPAs and their impact on learning. *J Grad Med Educ.*
   2023;15(6):676–684.
7. **Lockyer J, Carraccio C, Chan M-K, et al.** Core principles of assessment in competency-based
   medical education. *Med Teach.* 2017;39(6):609–616. — *the often-cited CBME assessment

8. **LaDonna KA, Hatala R, Lingard L, Voyer S, Watling C.** Staging a performance: learners'
   perceptions about direct observation during residency. *Med Educ.* 2017;51(5):498–510. — *direct
   observation can provoke a "textbook" inauthentic performance.*
9. **Martin L, Sibbald M, Brandt Vegas D, Russell D, Govaerts M.** The impact of entrustment
   assessments on feedback and learning: trainee perspectives. *Med Educ.* 2020;54(4):328–336.
10. **Bindal T, Wall D, Goodyear HM.** Trainee doctors' views on workplace-based assessments: are
    they just a tick box exercise? *Med Teach.* 2011;33(11):919–927.

## 6. Pearce — "When I say … assessment burden" (the recent Pearce paper)

**Citation:** Pearce J. When I say … assessment burden. *Medical Education.* 2025.
doi:10.1111/medu.15708. *(Volume/issue/pages to confirm — Crossref was not reachable from this
environment; confirmed via web search. "When I say…" is Medical Education's short conceptual
series.)*
**Status:** Not stored as a file (published article behind a paywall); referenced here.

**Why it matters:** This is the recent, directly-on-point conceptual piece by **Jacob Pearce**
(ACER — lead author of the ACER RANZCP reviews, §2/§3 above). It argues that **assessment burden
is not simply something to minimise**: high-quality assessment can *feel* burdensome yet carry
real utility for learning and standards — so the goal is to optimise *worthwhile* burden, not
eliminate burden per se. The natural conceptual companion to the RANZCP BoA literature review
(§5) and the strongest single citation for framing the AMC Condition 23 response.

**Related RANZCP output:** *Burden of assessment in the RANZCP Fellowship Program* (RANZCP survey
report; trainee/supervisor survey, Aug–Sep 2024; ~17.3% trainee response). College PDF at
`ranzcp.org/getmedia/…/Burden-of-assessment-in-the-RANZCP-Fellowship-Program.pdf` and news
summary "Burden of assessments – survey results." *(ranzcp.org blocks automated fetching — could
not retrieve/store the PDF here; please supply a copy if you want it stored and summarised.)*

## Cross-reference

These reports are the **RANZCP-internal evidentiary base** behind the assessment narrative in the
[main sectioned report](../redesign-report.md) — especially §4 (Assessment) and §8 (Equity &
Cultural Safety). The Schuwirth (2022) and ACER (2020) reviews independently make the case that a
single high-stakes OSCE is the wrong tool for psychiatric competence and that **programmatic,
judgement-based, longitudinal assessment** is more defensible — the same conclusion reached from
the published literature (Hodges; van der Vleuten & Schuwirth; ten Cate & Regehr).
